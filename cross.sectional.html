PH 320 / 01 / STUDY DESIGN

Cross-Sectional Studies & Causal Claims

Understanding what a snapshot of a population can—and cannot—tell us.

Mahmud Hasan · York College, CUNY

The researchers claimed that regular physical activity reduces depression in adults. They also recommended that counties invest in exercise programs to improve mental health.

Between March and May 2021, researchers collected completed questionnaires from 4,200 adults in one metropolitan county. Each person answered once, reporting exercise during the past week and depressive symptoms during the past two weeks. Frequent depressive symptoms were reported by 11% of those exercising at least four days a week, compared with 27% of those exercising once a week or less.

The researchers found a connection, but they did not show that exercise caused fewer depressive symptoms. Both answers came from the same survey, so they could not establish which came first. People with fewer depressive symptoms might exercise more, while people experiencing depression might find exercise difficult. Other factors, such as physical health or income, could also explain the relationship.

Cross-sectional studies collect information at one point in time. They can show how common a condition is and identify relationships between factors. By themselves, they cannot establish cause and effect or show how a person’s condition changes over time.

“Among adults who completed this survey, more frequent exercise was associated with fewer reported depressive symptoms. This study cannot establish whether exercise caused that difference.”
