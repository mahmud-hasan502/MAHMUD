PH 320 / 02 / REGRESSION ANALYSIS

Smoking & Psychological Distress

Interpreting the relationship through correlation, slope, and statistical significance.

Mahmud Hasan · York College, CUNY

  1. Correlation: The correlation between smoking and psychological distress is +0.77, which indicates a strong positive relationship. This means areas with higher smoking rates tend to have higher psychological distress rates.
  2. Slope: The slope is +0.4912, meaning that for every 1 percentage-point increase in smoking rates, psychological distress is predicted to increase by approximately 0.49 percentage points.
  3. P-value: The p-value is less than 0.001, which is below 0.05. This means the relationship is statistically significant.

What it means for people: Smoking and psychological distress have a strong positive relationship (r = +0.77). The slope of 0.4912 means that for every 1 percentage-point increase in smoking, psychological distress is predicted to increase by about 0.49 percentage points. The p-value is less than 0.001, which means the relationship is statistically significant.

Interpretation note

This analysis describes an association between smoking rates and psychological distress. Correlation and statistical significance alone do not establish that one causes the other.
